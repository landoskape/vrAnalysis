What does the tight scatter mean?

Let a be a cell's trial RMS amplitudes over T trials. The normalized participation ratio is (sum a)^2 / (T sum a^2) = mean(a)^2 / mean(a^2). With population variance, CV^2 = variance(a)/mean(a)^2, hence fraction active = 1 / (1 + RMS CV^2). Equivalently RMS CV = sqrt(1/fraction active - 1). This is an algebraic identity, not evidence for a separate mechanism. The maximum absolute difference from the identity among matched positive finite pairs across this cache is 8.49e-07; per-session audits are downloadable below.

The implementation first normalizes RMS amplitudes to sum to approximately 1, then computes normalized participation ratio. That normalization cancels algebraically. Multiplying every trial's amplitude by a constant leaves both participation and CV unchanged. Participation can rise because more trials are active, because existing active trials become more similar, or both. It is not a direct count of trials with events. A binary equal-amplitude on/off signal is a special case where participation equals the active-trial fraction.

The same identity holds if participation is computed separately from each other amplitude measure, but the stored fraction active uses RMS only. Therefore PF projection, mean-activity and Gaussian-gain CV are correlated with it rather than identically determined by it. Similarities across these amplitudes may reflect shared trial-to-trial modulation. These plots do not establish that recruitment alone explains the familiarity effect.

Activity measurements beyond participation

Mean trial RMS, mean activity across positions/trials, PF projection, PF peak and overall RMS measure amplitude and can change even with fixed participation. Mean Gaussian gain is a relative scale tied to its own template and is near 1 in these results, so it is not an activity-level measure.

Normalization limit: pfpred_quality divides each ROI's activity by its full-session standard deviation (and subtracts its median when configured). These figures show standardized activity levels within environments, not absolute fluorescence, event rates or firing rates. Temporal variance is also relative to full-session scale. Changing cell populations and a quality screen that includes participation can affect the curves.

To distinguish recruitment from amplitude stabilization, useful next measurements from trial-resolved data are: the actual fraction of trials exceeding a fixed noise-calibrated event threshold; amplitude conditional on exceeding that threshold; and mean unstandardized event signal or event count per running second. A fixed threshold in standardized units is possible but inherits the session normalization; counting any positive sigrebase activity would require checking its baseline/noise first. Conditional-amplitude and thresholded trial-fraction measures are not recoverable from the cached mean/variance/CV summaries alone.

Activity energy and participation: ROI median

Overall RMS is sqrt(mean(trial RMS)^2 + variance(trial RMS)) for each cell: the square root of mean squared trial RMS. It weights each valid trial equally and each finite position equally within trial; it is not a time-weighted firing rate. SD of trial RMS is sqrt(variance), an absolute variability measure rather than CV. Temporal variance within environment is normalized by full-session cell variance, so it measures relative allocation across environments. Participation is included as a reference. Same mouse weighting and minimum support as the amplitude figure.
