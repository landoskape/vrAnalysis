The shared-trial, held-out, and fully independent constructions answer different questions. Use the independent-trial result as the strongest check that mean–variability coupling generalizes across separate trials.

What is being measured?

For each neuron, PF is mean activity across trials at each position. RMS is the square root of the mean squared residual from a PF template at that position. The reported scalar is Pearson r across position between PF and RMS, not mean firing rate, temporal prediction RMS, or spatial reliability itself. A positive r means variability is higher where mean activity is higher; r measures map shape agreement, not proportionality or variance magnitude.

All-trial RMS uses deviations from the all-trial mean: this is a position-dependent population SD (ddof=0). F1 and F2 alternate chronological trials. Held-out averages r(PF F1, RMS on F2 relative to PF F1) and its reverse. The PF enters that residual, so it can contribute template-mismatch effects. Independent averages r(PF F1, within-F2 RMS relative to PF F2) and its reverse; each correlation’s two maps use disjoint trials. The two directional r values are averaged arithmetically, exactly as saved. Both directions must be finite.

Spatial reliability is the stored environment-specific reliability. Amplitude consistency is represented by across-trial CV (SD/mean); lower CV means more consistent amplitude. Trial RMS is the primary amplitude measure, with least-squares and Gaussian PF gain CV as alternatives. These CVs are not correlation coefficients and are not bounded by one.

Reading population statistics

Primary analysis uses each session’s environment with the most trials. Neurons are summarized within session; sessions receive equal weight within mouse; mice receive equal weight across the population. Thus large recordings and mice with many sessions do not dominate. All cells are included unless explicitly labeled. No cross-session cell matching is assumed: counts refer to ROI observations, not unique neurons. Bands/error bars are descriptive between-mouse SEM, not 95% confidence intervals. No neuron-level p-values are used.
